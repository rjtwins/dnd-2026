This is the Age of Chaos

Its history old, the empire had grown fat.

For a century peace had held, and with peace came wealth. The cities grew, the imperial court grew richer, and the great families accumulated land, titles and slaves. What had once been an empire built on conquest and law had become a place of indulgence.

Slavery was now common throughout the empire. The nobles cared little for the lands they ruled, leaving their estates to be managed by bureaucrats and servants. The bureaucrats themselves cared little for anything beyond their own wealth and position.

The legions had become more loyal to their commanders than to the emperor. Each general held armies, provinces and fortunes, and the emperors increasingly depended upon them to maintain their rule.

Then the emperor died.

Succession had never been peaceful, but it had always been decided in the end. This time there would be no such certainty.

Pretenders rose across the empire. Governors declared themselves emperors. Generals marched their legions against their rivals. Provinces rebelled, cities closed their gates, and old rivalries that had been buried for generations were brought back to the surface.

The civil war lasted for years.

One pretender after another marched on the imperial capital. Armies crossed the empire, fighting not for its people, but for the throne. Provinces changed allegiance again and again as generals proclaimed themselves the conqueror of old.

And then, when the empire was already exhausted, came the calamity.

By means unknown, the imperial capital and the verdant lands surrounding it were consumed by arcane fire. The heart of the empire now a blackened desert.

No army could claim victory. No emperor remained to rule. The survivors could do little but flee the ruins. 

The empire was shattered.

In the south, a remnant remained. The empire still existed in name, ruled by the great families who had survived the war. Their authority extended little beyond the lands they controlled, but they continued to call themselves imperial.

In the north, the provinces first split apart, and then those provinces themselves fractured. Kingdoms rose where governors had once ruled, cities declared themselves free, and petty lords carved their own realms from the remains of the old empire.

The empire was gone.

What remained was a land divided among those strong enough to claim it.
