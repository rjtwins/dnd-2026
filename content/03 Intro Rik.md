This is the Age of the Conqueror

Generations ago, humanity first came to Axandor Tor.

Arriving as refugees, crossing the sea from an ancient homeland they had abandoned in haste. Calamity had driven them from their old world so terrible that those who survived rarely spoke of it.

They landed upon unfamiliar shores and began again.

They founded Dawnport, the first great human settlement on the continent. From there, humanity spread slowly inland, establishing villages, towns, and eventually kingdoms across a land that had never been their own.

The years that followed were not peaceful.

Humanity's expansion brought them into conflict with the giants, beginning a war that would last for generations. In time, the dwarves joined the human struggle, and the alliance forged between the two peoples endured long after the giants were defeated.

With the giant threat broken, humanity turned its attention inward.

Kingdoms rose across Axandor Tor, each with its own crown, laws, ambitions, and borders. For centuries, these competed for territory and influence. Alliances were made and broken. Dynasties flourished and fell. Border disputes became wars, and wars became traditions passed from one generation to the next.

Nearly two centuries of conflict followed.
Then, a conqueror arose.

Kingdom by kingdom, the old order disappeared. Those who resisted were vanquished without mercy. Cities burned, kingdoms laid to waste.

Now stood the Empire.

The conqueror declared that the age of divided crowns had ended. The constant wars between the old kingdoms would cease, and all lands would answer to a single imperial authority.

For the first time since humanity settled Axandor Tor, its kingdoms were united under one banner.

Through conquest or integration other peoples living within its expanding borders were gradually incorporated into imperial society. Some entered willingly, seeking the stability and protection the new order provided. Others had little choice. Over time, the Empire became a realm of many peoples, bound together by imperial law rather than shared ancestry.

The dwarves remain allies of the Empire, as they have been allies of humanity for centuries. Their ancient pact has survived the rise of kings and now stands alongside the Empire itself.

To the north, the elven realms remain wary.

Relations between the Empire and the elven realms are governed by treaties, trade, and diplomacy, but neither side has forgotten the centuries of suspicion that preceded them. Peace exists between them, though it remains fragile.

There are roads where armies once marched. Borders that once shifted with every generation have been fixed. Trade moves farther than it ever has, and the peoples of the continent are more closely connected than they have ever been.

But the Empire was not built peacefully.

Old dynasties may have been erased. Rivalries have not disappeared simply because their kingdoms have. There are those who believe the Empire brought stability to a divided continent, and those who believe it merely replaced many tyrants with one.

Beyond the Empire's borders, much of Axandor Tor remains unknown. The ruins of older civilizations still lie beneath the wilderness. The scars of the giant wars remain. The elves guard histories that predate humanity's arrival, while the dwarves remember the alliance that helped shape the continent.

And far older still are the secrets carried across the sea by humanity's ancestors.

The calamity that drove them from their homeland has been forgotten by almost everyone.

The old world has become a distant memory.

Humanity has spent centuries building a new one.
Now, for the first time, nearly all of it belongs to the Empire.
